\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:rw}

Task scheduling in heterogeneous cloud datacenters has evolved from deterministic list-based prioritization to complex online learning frameworks. Traditional scheduling research focused on optimizing single-objective operational bounds, primarily makespan or throughput, using static low-level heuristics (LLHs) like HEFT and PEFT \citep{HosseiniShirvani2021A}. While computationally efficient with $O(V^2 E)$ complexity, these fixed-rule schedulers rely on static resource availability assumptions and suffer severe performance degradation when confronted with high workload variance or abrupt hardware faults. To address these search limitations, meta-heuristics like Genetic Algorithms and hybrid swarm optimizers were introduced \citep{Alkaam2025Hybrid}. Although meta-heuristics navigate multi-objective trade-offs effectively, their high computational overhead makes them unsuitable for real-time streaming task execution.

Recent advances have shifted toward data-driven paradigms, leveraging Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) formulations to enable online adaptability \citep{Khan2025Machine, DeCarvalho2025Toward}. DRL architectures achieve high optimization bounds in stationary environments; but they face significant practical hurdles: prolonged convergence times, high training overheads, and poor zero-shot generalization when faced with sudden distribution shifts \citep{Choppara2025Efficient, Sravan2026DRL}. Conversely, MAB hyper-heuristics offer lightweight online learning by dynamically selecting optimal LLHs based on real-time execution feedback rather than managing the continuous schedule space directly \citep{Singh2024Leveraging}.

Despite these advances, three critical research gaps persist in the current literature:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Historical Bias in Non-Stationary Environments:} Standard stationary MAB algorithms (such as UCB1 and standard Thompson Sampling) accumulate unweighted performance history. When unexpected node failures or capacity drops occur, these algorithms remain trapped in stale Q-values, causing prolonged adaptation lag before pivoting selection policies.
    \item \textbf{Lack of Lightweight Contextual Workload Fingerprinting:} Existing contextual or content-aware schedulers often rely on heavy offline classifiers or neural feature extractors \citep{Adil2022scp}. There is a lack of real-time, lightweight statistical fingerprinting mechanisms capable of dynamically modulating exploration parameters without introducing execution latency.
    \item \textbf{Unbalanced Multi-Objective Reward Formulations:} While sustainability and carbon intensity have emerged as first-class scheduling parameters alongside cost and latency \citep{Arora2023Towards}, existing multi-objective reward functions often suffer from scale distortion across heterogeneous execution batches, leading to single-metric dominance during bandit update steps.
\end{enumerate}

By integrating Discounted UCB (D-UCB) memory decay, Coefficient of Variation ($\text{CoV}$) trace fingerprinting, and a batch-relative, tanh-squashed multi-objective reward model, this approach achieves rapid adaptation under infrastructure failures while jointly optimizing runtime efficiency, operational cost, VM utilization, and carbon footprint.

The remainder of this section critically reviews prior contributions across these foundational domains.

\subsection{Foundations of Non-Stationary Workload Allocation}

Cloud computing provides scalable, on-demand infrastructure, but allocating tasks efficiently across virtual machines (VMs) remains difficult, because a scheduler must balance competing goals such as reducing makespan and cost, improving resource utilization, and limiting energy use as workloads change over time. The problem becomes harder in heterogeneous cloud environments, where task arrivals and resource demands are not stationary. Since task scheduling across heterogeneous resources is NP-hard, exact polynomial-time solutions are impractical at scale. This has led to the use of heuristics, meta-heuristics, and learning-based methods.

Early taxonomies, such as the study by \citet{Mathew2014Study}, grouped cloud allocation strategies around key performance measures including makespan, load balancing, and overall throughput. \citet{Saurav2021A} later extended this view to energy-aware workflow scheduling in large heterogeneous systems, highlighting the trade-offs between execution speed, resource contention, and power consumption. Together, these studies show exactly, why static, single-objective scheduling models struggle when workload arrivals and resource demands change over time.

\subsection{Deterministic Task Prioritization and Heuristic Approximations}

List-based schedulers such as HEFT, PEFT, RHEFT, and HSIP reduce the complexity of DAG scheduling through a two-stage process: rank the tasks, then assign them to processors. These methods provide polynomial-time approximations and predictable scheduling behaviour.

Building on this approach, \citet{HosseiniShirvani2021A} proposed HH-LiSch, a hybrid list scheduler that combines two ranking strategies, RankI and RankO, with critical-task duplication and idle-slot insertion. The method was evaluated on scientific workflow benchmarks including Montage, Epigenome, CyberShake, and LIGO. It reduced average makespan by 16.71\% compared with HEFT and PEFT.

The main limitation remains the same. Classical list schedulers use fixed rules and limited workload information. They do not learn from changing task characteristics or execution conditions.

\subsection{Content and Workload Feature Engineering for Scheduling}

Feature-informed schedulers address this limitation by using properties of the workload itself. Instead of treating every task as an abstract unit of computation, they examine task characteristics and use them to guide resource selection.

\citet{Adil2022scp} proposed CA-MLBS, which combines Support Vector Machine (SVM) classification with Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). The scheduler classifies incoming tasks by content type, including video, audio, image, and text, and then maps them to suitable VM configurations. In CloudSim experiments, CA-MLBS reduced makespan by up to 29\% and increased throughput by up to 44\% compared with feature-agnostic baselines. The results suggest that workload features can improve resource matching.

However, CA-MLBS still depends on a classifier trained offline. Once deployed, its routing policy does not adapt directly to unexpected changes in execution behaviour or workload conditions.

\subsection{Complex Search Space Exploration in Hybrid Meta-Heuristics}

Meta-heuristics such as Genetic Algorithms (GA), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), and Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) search large scheduling spaces without exhaustively evaluating every possible allocation. They can escape poor local solutions and often produce better schedules than simple rule-based policies.

For example, \citet{Kumar2021Implementation} applied ACO in CloudSim and reported lower average task execution times and cloudlet failure rates than Round Robin and Minimum Completion Time (MCT) policies.

Recent studies have gone further by combining several meta-heuristics. \citet{Alkaam2025Hybrid} proposed HGHHC, which combines Henry Gas Solubility Optimization, Harris Hawks Optimization, and Comprehensive Opposition-Based Learning (COBL). Across HPC2N, NASA, and synthetic workload traces, HGHHC reduced makespan by 34.30\%, 72.95\%, and 28.67\%, respectively, relative to the reported baseline swarm methods. \citet{Bal2022A} similarly proposed RATS-HM, combining an improved cat swarm optimization scheduler (ICS-TS), a group optimization deep neural network (GO-DNN), and NSUPREME encryption for resource allocation and security.

The drawback is computational cost. These methods often require repeated population-based searches to produce a schedule. That can work for offline optimization, but repeatedly running such searches as workloads change is expensive and may be too slow for online batch decisions.

\subsection{Adaptive Online Decision-Making and Regret Minimization}

Multi-Armed Bandits (MABs) offer a different approach. Instead of searching for an entire schedule from scratch, an MAB model treats scheduling choices as actions and learns which actions perform well as new observations arrive. The key trade-off is between exploration, where the scheduler tests less-known choices, and exploitation, where it uses choices that have performed well.

\citet{Singh2024Leveraging} formulated a game-theoretic MAB model for cloud resource allocation, deriving logarithmic regret bounds and reporting improvements in utilization, latency, and fairness over static baselines. Other studies have applied MABs to distributed and edge settings. \citet{Tran-Dang2024Parallel} combined Thompson Sampling with matching theory for decentralized fog task offloading through MAB-MATCH. \citet{Chigu2024A} developed a UCB-based scheduler for serverless edge-cloud FaaS and reported execution latencies close to an oracle policy across AWS Lambda and OpenWhisk. MAB methods have also been used for federated learning user selection under mobility constraints \citep{Jun2025Mobility} and joint satellite beamforming \citep{Liang2026Joint}.

The main advantage of this direction is adaptability. Unlike fixed heuristics or offline classifiers, MAB-based schedulers can update their decisions as new performance feedback arrives. This makes them a useful candidate for non-stationary workloads, where the best scheduling choice can change over time.
