\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:Conclusion}
In this study, we tried to answer the question of whether an adaptive Multi-Armed Bandit hyper-heuristic framework could consistently schedule tasks in a heterogeneous cloud environment in real time, while keeping track of multiple objectives such as makespan and energy consumption. The process of getting the answer involved developing a scheduling architecture that incorporates a range of scheduling algorithms, from list-based schedulers to hybrid algorithms. A reward metric that is the output of a function that computes the makespan, cost and carbon footprint of the simulated machines run within CloudSIm showed the viability of the framework.

The value of this work lies in establishing the Multi-Armed Bandit approach as a real and practical compromise between static list-based schedulers and more compute-heavy metaheuristics. This approach helps to eliminate the need for offline training, and it enables automated adaptability. 

Future research should try and transition to a contextual Multi-Armed Bandit approach because it takes more into account before making a decision, as opposed to a context-free MAB that goes for the globally best option. Opting for a Deep Reinforcement Learning approach is also an avenue to explore, as the decisions made in a round carry delayed consequences in future rounds.
